# Voice brief template

Copy this, fill it from transcripts, delete the brackets. Every `[ ]` should either
be replaced or deleted — a brief full of placeholders teaches nothing.

```markdown
# [Name] — voice

Channel: [handle]. [What the channel is about, in one line.]

## Who is talking
[Role and audience: who is this person to the viewer, and what have they actually
done? Not a job title — the thing that earns them the right to explain this.]

## Delivery
- energy: [flat and even / animated / dry]
- range: [can they do emphasis, warmth, sarcasm on demand? if not, say so]
- pace: [normal / fast / leaves gaps]
- pronunciation: [product names, spelled how they say them]

## Voice traits
1. [Behaviour] — yes: "[example line]" / no: "[the generic version]"
2. …
(5–8. Each one has to be observable in a transcript.)

## Sentence mechanics
- length: [range], complete connected sentences, never fragments
- joins: [how they connect related facts — comma, "and", a relative clause]
- opens with: [markers they actually use]
- person: [first/second], tense: [past for events, present for facts]
- contractions: [always / never / mixed]

## Vocabulary
uses: [10–20 words and phrases that are genuinely theirs]
refers to things as: [e.g. "the box", not "the server"]

## Never uses
[Personal additions to the ban list: words that feel fake in their mouth, brand or
legal terms, competitor names, anything they have cut more than twice.]

## Structures
- opens on: [result / failure / claim]
- explains by: [story / walkthrough / analogy]
- ends on: [concrete line / next action / callback]

## Reference passages
> "[verbatim excerpt]"
why: [the specific move — rhythm, bluntness, specificity, the admission before the
correction]

> "[verbatim excerpt]"
why: …

## Good vs bad
Good: [what it sounds like when it works]
Bad: [the failure mode — "reads like a LinkedIn post", "reads like a press release"]
```

## Filling it quickly

1. Drop 2–3 transcripts into the conversation.
2. Ask for the traits, not the adjectives: *"find the repeated sentence shapes and
   the words I use when I'm explaining vs when I'm admitting something went wrong."*
3. Check each trait against a line you can point at. If you can't find the line, the
   trait is invented.
4. Write the reference passages by hand. They are worth more than the rest of the
   document combined.
